Equilibrium biomass

Equilibrium-based community models need the biomass of each species. FoodWebs.jl reads it from data where a web has it, or computes it from steady-state energy flow through the web.

Observed biomass

Webs read from data keep the extra columns of their nodes.csv as fw.traits. For the cheddar webs with body mass M and numerical abundance N, biomass is their product; species without measurements, such as detritus, are missing.

using FoodWebs

fw = foodweb("cheddar/tl84")
x_observed = biomass(fw)
extrema(x_observed)
(6.0e-8, 0.0036)

Steady-state energy flow

steady_state_biomass passes production up the web: basal species produce at a fixed rate, each consumer produces a transfer efficiency times the production it takes from its prey, and biomass is production divided by turnover. It needs no trophic levels, so omnivory and feeding loops are handled by the web itself. With turnover scaling as M^(-1/4), the biomass ratio per level is TE · R^(1/4) for a predator–prey mass ratio R, so pyramids invert when TE · R^(1/4) > 1 (McCauley et al. 2018).

x_flow = steady_state_biomass(fw; efficiency = 0.1, turnover = :allometric)
pyramid_slope(fw, x_flow), pyramid_slope(fw, x_observed)
(-0.12439311049831557, 0.5682133805860158)

The slope of log biomass against trophic level is negative for the energy-flow prediction and positive for the observed biomass: Tuesday Lake is top-heavy, with more biomass at higher levels than steady-state transfer at 10% efficiency and allometric turnover gives.

Pyramid measures

biomass_ratios gives the consumer-to-resource ratio on every link, pyramid_slope the mean change per trophic level, and is_pyramid and is_inverted_pyramid whether biomass never rises (or never falls) up a link.

FoodWebs.biomass — Function
biomass(fw::FoodWeb)

The observed biomass of each species, body mass times numerical abundance, from the traits "M" and "N" of fw, in the units of the web's catalog entry (fw.metadata["properties"]). Species without a measured mass or abundance, such as detritus, have missing. Throws an ArgumentError if the web has no "M" or "N" trait.

FoodWebs.steady_state_biomass — Function
steady_state_biomass([rng], web; efficiency = 0.1, turnover = 1.0, basal_production = 1.0,
                     shares = :equal, turnover_exponent = -1/4, noise = 0.0)

Equilibrium biomasses from steady-state energy flow through web. Each basal species produces basal_production; each consumer j produces efficiency[j] times the production it takes from its prey, a share D[i, j] of each prey i's production, so that

P = P_basal + diag(efficiency) Dᵀ P,

and each species' biomass is its production divided by its turnover (production per unit biomass), x = P ./ turnover. No trophic levels are needed, and webs with loops are allowed.

  • efficiency: the transfer efficiency, production of a consumer per unit of production it eats, a number or one per species, in (0, 1).
  • turnover: a number, one per species, or :allometric for M^turnover_exponent from the body mass trait "M" of a FoodWeb (metabolic scaling, exponent -1/4 by default).
  • basal_production: a number, or one per species (used for basal species).
  • shares: :equal to split each prey's production equally among its predators, or a matrix D[prey, predator] that is zero off the feeding links and sums to at most 1 over each prey's predators.
  • noise: each biomass is multiplied by exp(noise * z) with z standard normal from rng.

Cannibalistic links are left out. With a constant efficiency TE along a food chain and turnover falling by a factor k per level, biomass changes by TE * k per level: with allometric turnover and a constant predator–prey mass ratio R, by TE * R^(1/4), so the pyramid inverts when TE * R^(1/4) > 1, the conditions McCauley et al. (2018) give for top-heavy communities. Throws an ArgumentError for species with no feeding path up from a basal species (see disconnected_from_basal), which receive no production.

McCauley, D. J. et al. On the prevalence and dynamics of inverted trophic pyramids and otherwise top-heavy communities. Ecol. Lett. 21, 439–454 (2018).

FoodWebs.biomass_ratios — Function
biomass_ratios(web, x)

The consumer-to-resource biomass ratio x[predator] / x[prey] on each feeding link of web, as named tuples (prey, predator, ratio), leaving out cannibalism. Ratios near or above 1 mark top-heavy links (McCauley et al. 2018).

source
FoodWebs.pyramid_slope — Function
pyramid_slope(web, x; levels = trophic_levels(web))

The least-squares slope of log.(x) against the trophic levels, so that exp of the slope is the mean biomass ratio per trophic level: below 1 for an Eltonian pyramid, above 1 for an inverted one. Biomasses must be positive.

source
FoodWebs.is_pyramid — Function
is_pyramid(web, x)

Whether every consumer's biomass is at most each of its prey's, along the feeding links of web (leaving out cannibalism): biomass never increases up a link. Webs with feeding loops cannot satisfy this strictly unless the species on a loop have equal biomass.

source
FoodWebs.is_inverted_pyramid — Function
is_inverted_pyramid(web, x)

Whether every consumer's biomass is at least each of its prey's, along the feeding links of web (leaving out cannibalism): biomass never decreases up a link.
