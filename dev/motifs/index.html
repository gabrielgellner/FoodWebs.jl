Motifs

A motif is a connected subgraph of a few species, counted up to relabeling. motif_census counts the 13 three-species motifs or the 199 four-species classes, labeled by the mfinder IDs of Milo et al. (2002). The three-species motifs carry the names of Stouffer et al. (2007): S1 food chain, S2 omnivory, S3 loop, S4 exploitative competition, S5 apparent competition, and D1–D8 with two-way links. The four-species diamond, two consumers sharing a prey and a predator, is M904.

using FoodWebs

web = foodweb("williams_martinez_2000/bridge_brook_lake")
Dict(motif_name(id) => n for (id, n) in zip(motif_ids(3), motif_census(web)) if n > 0)

Over- and under-representation

motif_zscores compares the census with webs randomized by rewire_links, which keeps each species' numbers of prey, predators, one-way and two-way links, and its cannibalism. All eight bundled webs studied by Stouffer et al. (2007) fall in their published groups by the signs of the S2, S4 and S5 z-scores.

using Random

p = motif_zscores(Xoshiro(1), web; samples = 100)
Dict(motif_name(id) => round(z, digits = 1) for (id, z) in zip(p.ids, p.z) if isfinite(z))

Species roles

motif_roles counts each species' occurrences in the 30 positions of the three-species motifs (Stouffer et al. 2012), such as the top, middle and bottom of a food chain.

FoodWebs.motif_census — Function
motif_census(web; size = 3)

The number of times each connected motif of size species (3 or 4) occurs in web as an induced subgraph, in the order of motif_ids(size), where web[i, j] != 0 means species j eats species i. Cannibalistic links are left out. The counts sum to the number of weakly connected sets of size species.

source
FoodWebs.motif_ids — Function
motif_ids(size)

The mfinder IDs of the connected motifs of size species, in increasing order: 13 for size = 3 and 199 for size = 4. motif_census counts motifs in this order.

source
FoodWebs.motif_name — Function
motif_name(id)

The name of the motif with mfinder ID id: the Stouffer et al. (2007) names for the 13 three-species motifs ("S1" food chain, "S2" omnivory, "S3" loop, "S4" exploitative competition, "S5" apparent competition, "D1" to "D8" with two-way links), and "M$id" for the others. The four-species diamond, two consumers sharing a prey and a predator, is "M904".

source
FoodWebs.motif_roles — Function
motif_roles(web)

The number of times each species occupies each of the 30 positions in the three-species motifs (Stouffer et al. 2012), as an S×30 matrix. Position r is FoodWebs.ROLE_KEYS[r]: the motif's mfinder ID and the species' numbers of predators and prey within the motif, in the order used by pymfinder. For example position 1 is the top of a food chain (S1, no predators, one prey) and position 3 its bottom. Each motif occurrence adds one count to each of its three species, so the matrix sums to three times the number of connected sets of three species.

source
FoodWebs.rewire_links — Function
rewire_links([rng], web; swaps = 10 * L)

A randomized copy of web with the same number of prey and predators for every species and the same numbers of one-way and two-way links per species (Milo et al. 2002; Stouffer et al. 2007), for the null model of motif_zscores. Each of swaps attempts picks two one-way links a → b and c → d (a eats b) and rewires them to a → d and c → b, or two two-way links and rewires them likewise, rejecting swaps that would duplicate a link or join two linked species. Cannibalistic links are kept as they are. L is the number of links.

source
FoodWebs.motif_zscores — Function
motif_zscores([rng], web; size = 3, samples = 100, swaps = 10 * L)

How over- or under-represented each motif of size species is in web compared with samples randomized webs from rewire_links. Returns a named tuple with the motif ids, the observed counts, the mean and sd of the randomized counts, the z-scores z = (observed - mean) / sd, which are NaN for motifs whose randomized count never varies, and the normalized profile: the z-scores scaled to length 1, with NaN taken as 0 (Milo et al. 2004). Profiles compare webs of different sizes, as in Stouffer et al. (2007), and the dot product of two profiles is their uncentred correlation.

Milo, R. et al. Superfamilies of evolved and designed networks. Science 303, 1538–1542 (2004).

Stouffer, D. B., Camacho, J., Jiang, W. & Amaral, L. A. N. Evidence for the existence of a robust pattern of prey selection in food webs. Proc. R. Soc. B 274, 1931–1940 (2007).
