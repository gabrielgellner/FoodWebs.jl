Compartments

Compartments are groups of species that interact more among themselves than with others. In real food webs they are mostly energy channels and habitat splits, and their modularity is close to what the generalized niche model predicts (Guimerà et al. 2010). They limit how far extinctions and perturbations spread (Stouffer & Bascompte 2011), while the effect of block structure on the eigenvalues of a community matrix depends on the sign and strength of the links between the compartments (Grilli, Rogers & Allesina 2016).

modularity scores a partition of a web, or of a weight matrix such as energy fluxes, by how many more links fall inside the groups than expected from the species' degrees. Its directed form (Leicht & Newman 2008) is the default; the undirected form of Newman & Girvan (2004) is the one most published food web values use.

using FoodWebs

# two food chains, 1 → 2 → 3 and 4 → 5 → 6, joined by a top predator 7 that eats 3 and 6
web = falses(7, 7)
for (prey, predator) in [(1, 2), (2, 3), (4, 5), (5, 6), (3, 7), (6, 7)]
    web[prey, predator] = true
end
channels = [1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3]
layers = [1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3, 3]
modularity(web, channels), modularity(web, layers)
(0.3333333333333333, 3.700743415417188e-17)

The two channels score 1/3. Grouping by trophic layer scores no better than chance here, as every link runs between the groups. Grouping the Tuesday Lake web by the category of each species (producers, invertebrates and vertebrate ectotherms), which follows its trophic layers, scores below zero:

fw = foodweb("cheddar/tl84")
modularity(fw, fw.traits["category"]), modularity(fw, fw.traits["category"]; directed = false)
(-0.05558425160697913, -0.22634727961432433)

The resolution γ (Reichardt & Bornholdt 2006) sets the size of the groups that modularity favours; values above 1 favour smaller groups. The modularity of a web's best partition is never negative and is high even for random webs (Guimerà, Sales-Pardo & Amaral 2004), so its significance has to be judged against null webs.

FoodWebs.modularity — Function
modularity(W, groups; directed = true, γ = 1)

The modularity of the partition groups (one label per species) of the web or non-negative weight matrix W, where W[i, j] is the link from prey i to predator j, such as an energy flux. Cannibalistic links (the diagonal) are left out.

With directed = true, the directed modularity of Leicht & Newman (2008), weighted as in Arenas et al. (2007),

Q = (1/W) Σ_ij [W_ij − γ s_i^out s_j^in / W] δ(g_i, g_j),

where s_i^out = Σ_j W_ij, s_j^in = Σ_i W_ij and W = Σ_ij W_ij; Q is the same for W and its transpose, so the direction convention does not matter. With directed = false, the modularity of Newman & Girvan (2004) on the undirected web W + Wᵀ,

Q = (1/2m) Σ_ij [A_ij − γ k_i k_j / 2m] δ(g_i, g_j),

in which each link counts once, so that two species that eat each other are joined by two links. γ is the resolution of Reichardt & Bornholdt (2006): larger values favour smaller groups. Q is 0 for a single group and for a web without links.

Leicht, E. A. & Newman, M. E. J. Community structure in directed networks. Phys. Rev. Lett. 100, 118703 (2008). Arenas, A., Duch, J., Fernández, A. & Gómez, S. Size reduction of complex networks preserving modularity. New J. Phys. 9, 176 (2007). Newman, M. E. J. & Girvan, M. Finding and evaluating community structure in networks. Phys. Rev. E 69, 026113 (2004). Reichardt, J. & Bornholdt, S. Statistical mechanics of community detection. Phys. Rev. E 74, 016110 (2006).
