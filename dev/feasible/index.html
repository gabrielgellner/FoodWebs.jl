Feasible communities

Lotka–Volterra dynamics dxᵢ/dt = xᵢ (rᵢ + Σⱼ Aᵢⱼ xⱼ) have the equilibrium x > 0 when r + A x = 0. At a fixed x and web this is linear in the interaction strengths and in r, so the parameters consistent with the equilibrium, with linear ecological constraints, form a polytope. FeasibleCommunities describes it.

The coordinates are a loss and a gain magnitude for each feeding link, a self-regulation magnitude for each self-regulated species, and the growth rates when they are given as intervals (the default). Species that eat each other get a loss and a gain at each of their entries of A, and cannibalism merges into self-regulation, so every web can be used.

using FoodWebs, Random

rng = Xoshiro(2026)
web = generalized_cascade_network(rng, 15, 0.15; basal_paths = true)
x = steady_state_biomass(web; efficiency = 0.2)
fc = FeasibleCommunities(web, x; efficiency = (0.0, 0.3), per_capita_bounds = (0.0, 5.0))
FeasibleCommunities on 15 species with 96 coordinates
dimension(fc), isbounded(fc), structurally_singular(fc), diagnose(fc)
(81, true, false, String[])

Constraints

  • rates: fixed growth rates, or intervals by role (basal species in [0, 5] and consumers in [-1, 0] by default), which makes them coordinates.
  • self_regulation: which species have a self-regulation term (:all by default, as a magnitude of 0 is allowed; :all_but_top reproduces Gellner, McCann & Hastings).
  • efficiency: a band e_min λ ≤ γ ≤ e_max λ on each link's gain against its loss.
  • self_share: self-regulation as a share of each species' turnover.
  • per_capita_bounds: a box on the per capita rate of every loss, gain and self-regulation term, on the scale of the growth rates.
  • bounds: a box on every magnitude.

halfspaces gives the polytope as plain arrays, lower ≤ θ ≤ upper, A θ ≤ b, C θ = d, and interaction_matrix, growth_rates, community_matrix and fluxes turn a point into a model.

How the biomasses enter

The community matrix J = diag(x) A has the eigenvalues of B = A diag(x), whose entry A_ij x_j is the per capita effect of species j on the growth rate of species i, and the equilibrium r + A x = 0 is r + B 1 = 0. The rate intervals, the equilibrium, self_share and per_capita_bounds constrain B without reference to x, so:

  • rescaling x by a constant leaves the spectra unchanged;
  • without efficiency bands, the spectra do not depend on x at all.

With efficiency bands, x matters, and which constraint carries it depends on the coordinates. In B, the band on a link where predator j eats prey i bounds the per capita gain over the per capita loss by e x_i / x_j. J is also similar to M = diag(√x) A diag(√x), in which the band bounds the gain over the loss by e whatever x is, and x enters through the equilibrium M √x = -diag(√x) r.

Magnitude bounds add a dependence on the scale and shape of x: a bound hi on a coordinate at A[i, j] caps its per capita rate at hi x_j. per_capita_bounds bound the interactions on the scale of the growth rates instead. The tests check the scale invariance, and the equivalence of a pyramid with per capita efficiency bands on x = 1, on sampled points.

Structure without a solver

dimension counts the free directions, isbounded decides boundedness from the web and the constraints (exactly, as the tests check against linear programs), structurally_singular finds webs whose interaction matrix is singular at every point, and diagnose lists violated necessary conditions, such as an energy budget that cannot balance:

chain = food_chain_network(2)
diagnose(FeasibleCommunities(chain, [1.0, 1.0]; rates = [0.1, -1.0], efficiency = 0.5))
1-element Vector{String}:
 "with efficiency bands the budge" ⋯ 39 bytes ⋯ "egative for every allowed rate"

Testing whether the polytope has any point, and sampling it, need a linear program and a sampler, which the PolytopeSamplers.jl extension provides (see Sampling).

Priors

A uniform distribution on the polytope is one prior. CommunityPrior puts a distribution on each kind of coordinate, and logdensity gives the density restricted to the polytope, which a sampler for non-uniform targets uses. A density on the growth rates times a uniform on the interaction strengths conditions a joint prior on the equilibrium holding, weighting each rate vector by the volume of interaction strengths it allows; drawing the rates first and sampling the interactions on their slice does not, so the two priors differ. A uniform distribution stays uniform under linear changes of coordinates, so uniform in the magnitudes is uniform in the fluxes or in the entries of the community matrix.

Sampling

Loading PolytopeSamplers.jl enables PolytopeSamplers.Polytope(fc), isempty(fc) (a linear program), sample_communities and feasible_growth_rates. sample_communities runs chains until the effective sample size reaches target_ess, with the billiard walk for the uniform distribution and coordinate hit-and-run for a prior, which was 2 to 3.5 times faster than CRHMC on webs of 63 and 210 dimensions.

using PolytopeSamplers
using LinearAlgebra: eigvals

isempty(fc)
false
res = sample_communities(rng, fc; target_ess = 200)
stable = count(c -> maximum(real, eigvals(c.J)) < 0, res.communities) / length(res.communities)
res.converged, stable
(true, 0.86175)

The published constrained food web studies fix the growth rates instead, drawing them by role with draw_growth_rates and redrawing until the polytope has a point; feasible_growth_rates runs this loop. With magnitude bounds the biomasses must be on a scale where the rates divided by them fit within the bounds; per capita bounds fit at any scale:

found = feasible_growth_rates(rng, web, x; efficiency = (0.0, 0.3), per_capita_bounds = (0.0, 5.0))
found.draws, dimension(found.communities)
(1, 66)

Three ensembles

The same web gives three ensembles of community matrices, each assuming more than the last:

  1. random_predprey draws the link magnitudes independently, with a fixed diagonal and no equilibrium. Its stability depends on the web's sign structure and the magnitudes' moments (see elliptic_law).
  2. Feasible communities, J = diag(x) A for points of FeasibleCommunities(web, x), require the equilibrium x, which ties each row of A to the growth rate and biomass of its species.
  3. Feasible and energetic communities add an efficiency band, so that no consumer gains more than a share of what its prey lose.
using Distributions: Uniform

stability(Js) = count(J -> maximum(real, eigvals(J)) < 0, Js) / length(Js)
unconstrained = stability([random_predprey(rng, web, Uniform(0, 1), 0.3) for _ in 1:1000])
feasible = sample_communities(rng, FeasibleCommunities(web, x; per_capita_bounds = (0.0, 5.0)); target_ess = 200)
energetic = sample_communities(rng, FeasibleCommunities(web, x; efficiency = (0.0, 0.3), per_capita_bounds = (0.0, 5.0)); target_ess = 200)
(; unconstrained, feasible = stability([c.J for c in feasible.communities]),
    energetic = stability([c.J for c in energetic.communities]))
(unconstrained = 1.0, feasible = 0.859, energetic = 0.8565)

The gallery note gallery/equilibria.qmd compares the feasible ensembles across biomass pyramids, from Eltonian to inverted, and on the observed biomasses of the Tuesday Lake webs.

Tilting towards weak or strong interactions

An ExponentialTilt weights each coordinate of a kind by exp(-rate θ): a positive rate tilts towards weak interactions and a negative rate towards strong ones, which no distribution on [0, ∞) can express. Scaling the rate of each coordinate by its uniform mean, from mean_coordinates, skews every interaction by the same amount relative to its scale. Priors made of tilts and exponential distributions are sampled exactly on each chord, faster and more reliably than a general density; strong tilts push the density towards the faces of the polytope and still need more steps than the uniform:

means = mean_coordinates(rng, fc; target_ess = 200)
losses = findall(c -> c.kind === :loss, coordinates(fc))
map((-1.0, 2.0)) do λ
    prior = CommunityPrior(; loss = ExponentialTilt(λ ./ means.loss), gain = ExponentialTilt(λ ./ means.gain))
    tilted = sample_communities(rng, fc, prior; target_ess = 200)
    ratio = sum(tilted.points[losses, :]; dims = 2) ./ size(tilted.points, 2) ./ means.loss
    (; λ, mean_ratio = round(sum(ratio) / length(ratio); digits = 2), tilted.converged)
end
((λ = -1.0, mean_ratio = 1.27, converged = true), (λ = 2.0, mean_ratio = 0.54, converged = true))
FoodWebs.sample_communities — Function
sample_communities([rng], fc::FeasibleCommunities[, prior::CommunityPrior]; target_ess = 1000,
                   sampler, exact = true, kwargs...)

Sample the polytope of fc, uniformly or from the density of prior, with PolytopeSamplers.jl (load it to enable this method), running chains until their smallest effective sample size reaches target_ess with R̂ below 1.01. The default sampler is the billiard walk for the uniform distribution and coordinate hit-and-run for a prior (BilliardWalk samples only uniform targets); CRHMC uses the gradient of the prior's log density. Other keywords go to PolytopeSamplers.sample_until_converged.

When every coordinate's prior is nothing, an ExponentialTilt or an Exponential on a coordinate that cannot be negative, the density is exp(-cᵀθ) on the polytope, and with exact = true it is sampled as a PolytopeSamplers.ExponentialTarget, which hit-and-run draws exactly on each chord, several times faster than the slice sampling of a general density and more reliable in convergence. exact = false uses the general density. A tilt with rate ≤ 0 on an unbounded polytope throws an ArgumentError, as its density does not normalize.

Returns a named tuple: points, a matrix with one sampled point per column after burn-in, pooled over chains; communities, a named tuple (A, r, J) for each point; diagnostics; and converged. Throws an ArgumentError if the polytope is empty or unbounded.

FoodWebs.mean_coordinates — Function
mean_coordinates([rng], fc::FeasibleCommunities; target_ess = 1000, kwargs...)

The means of the coordinates of fc under the uniform distribution, from sample_communities (load PolytopeSamplers.jl to enable this method): a named tuple with the mean of every coordinate, coordinate, and the means of the coordinates of each kind, in the order of coordinates, loss, gain, self and growth. Use them to match a prior to the uniform, such as ExponentialTilt(λ ./ means.loss).

FoodWebs.ExponentialTilt — Type
ExponentialTilt(rate)

A prior for one kind of coordinate in a CommunityPrior, with density proportional to exp(-rate * θ) for each coordinate θ of that kind, restricted to the polytope: rate = 0 is uniform, rate > 0 tilts towards weak interactions and rate < 0 towards strong ones. rate is a number for every coordinate of the kind, or a vector with one rate for each coordinate of the kind, in the order of coordinates. Unlike Exponential, any sign is allowed, as the density need only normalize on the polytope, which is bounded unless the constraints leave it open (see isbounded); sampling a tilt with a rate ≤ 0 on an unbounded polytope throws an ArgumentError.

The tilt exp(-λ Σ_k θ_k / μ_k), with μ_k the uniform mean of each coordinate, skews the interaction strengths by λ relative to the scale of each: ExponentialTilt(λ ./ means.loss) with means = mean_coordinates(fc) (mean_coordinates). One rate for the whole kind weights coordinates with large uniform means far more strongly, concentrating the density and slowing the samplers.

FoodWebs.feasible_growth_rates — Function
feasible_growth_rates([rng], web, x; maxiter = 1000, basal, consumer, kwargs...)

Draw growth rates with draw_growth_rates until the FeasibleCommunities with those fixed rates (built with the other keywords) has a point, checked with a linear program through PolytopeSamplers.jl (load it to enable this method). Returns the rates r, the communities and the number of draws, or throws an ErrorException after maxiter draws. This reproduces the redraw loop of the published studies; rates as coordinates need no redraws.

With magnitude bounds, the scale of x sets whether a draw can be feasible: each row of r + A x = 0 divides a rate by biomasses, so with biomasses much smaller than the rates the magnitudes exceed the bounds. per_capita_bounds bound the interactions on the scale of the rates and do not depend on x.

source
FoodWebs.draw_growth_rates — Function
draw_growth_rates([rng], web; basal = Uniform(0, 5), consumer = Uniform(0, 1))

Growth rates drawn by role: r_i ~ basal for species that eat nothing and r_i = -c, c ~ consumer, for the rest, as in the published constrained food web studies. Fixed rates drawn this way and then a uniform distribution on the interaction strengths give a different prior from rates as coordinates of FeasibleCommunities (see CommunityPrior).

FoodWebs.FeasibleCommunities — Type
FeasibleCommunities(web, x; rates, self_regulation = :all, efficiency = nothing,
                    self_share = nothing, bounds = (0.0, Inf), per_capita_bounds = nothing)

The Lotka–Volterra parameters on the food web web (web[i, j] != 0 when species j eats species i) that have the equilibrium biomasses x > 0: the interaction matrix A and growth rates r with r + A x = 0, under linear ecological constraints. They form a polytope in the coordinates listed by coordinates: a loss and a gain magnitude for each feeding link, a self-regulation magnitude for each self-regulated species, and the growth rates when they are variables.

  • rates: a vector of fixed growth rates, or intervals that make the rates coordinates: a named tuple (basal = (lo, hi), consumer = (lo, hi)) by role (basal species eat nothing), or a vector of (lo, hi) pairs. The default, (basal = (0.0, 5.0), consumer = (-1.0, 0.0)), matches the draws of the published constrained food web studies.
  • self_regulation: the species with a self-regulation term: :all (the default; a magnitude of 0 is allowed, so this includes no self-regulation, but each term adds a dimension and changes a uniform prior), :all_but_top (species no other eats, as in Gellner, McCann & Hastings), :basal, :none, or a vector of booleans. Cannibals always have one, which holds their net cannibalism.
  • efficiency: nothing, a number e, a band (e_min, e_max), or a matrix or pair of matrices over [prey, predator], constraining each link's gain to e_min λ ≤ γ ≤ e_max λ with 0 ≤ e_min ≤ e_max ≤ 1.
  • self_share: nothing, a share s or a band (s_min, s_max) in [0, 1] of each self-regulated species' turnover taken by its self-regulation, s_min T_i ≤ x_i σ_i ≤ s_max T_i.
  • bounds: (lo, hi) for every loss, gain and self-regulation magnitude.
  • per_capita_bounds: nothing, or (lo, hi) for the per capita rate of every loss, gain and self-regulation term: a coordinate θ at A[i, j] contributes θ x[j] to species i's per capita growth rate, so the bound is lo / x[j] ≤ θ ≤ hi / x[j]. It applies together with bounds.

The community matrix J = diag(x) A has the eigenvalues of A diag(x), the matrix of per capita rates, and the equilibrium, the rate intervals, self_share and per_capita_bounds constrain per capita rates only. Rescaling x by a constant therefore leaves the spectra unchanged, and without efficiency bands so does any change of x. A finite bounds caps the per capita rate of a coordinate at A[i, j] at hi x[j], which depends on the scale and the shape of x. Use per_capita_bounds to bound the interactions on the scale of the growth rates.

Species that eat each other get a loss and a gain at each of their two entries of A; only the difference enters the equilibrium, so each such pair adds directions that the equilibrium leaves to the prior. See halfspaces for the constraints, dimension, isbounded, structurally_singular and diagnose for their structure, and interaction_matrix, growth_rates and community_matrix for the model at a point.

FoodWebs.coordinates — Function
coordinates(fc::FeasibleCommunities)

One named tuple (kind, i, j) per coordinate of the polytope, in order: kind is :loss (prey i's loss to predator j, at A[i, j]), :gain (predator i's gain from prey j, at A[i, j]), :self (species i's self-regulation, at A[i, i]) or :growth (species i's growth rate, with j = i).

FoodWebs.halfspaces — Function
halfspaces(fc::FeasibleCommunities)

The polytope of fc as (lower, upper, A, b, C, d): lower .≤ θ .≤ upper, A θ .≤ b and C θ .== d, the form of PolytopeSamplers.jl, with θ in the order of coordinates. The equilibrium rows r + A x = 0 come first in C, then fixed efficiencies and shares; efficiency and share bands are in A.

The interaction matrix A at the point θ of fc: each entry the sum of its gain coordinates minus its loss coordinates, and minus the self-regulation on the diagonal.

FoodWebs.dimension — Function
dimension(fc::FeasibleCommunities)

The dimension of the affine hull of the equality constraints of fc: the number of coordinates less the rank of the equalities and of coordinates fixed by equal bounds. This is the dimension of the polytope when it has a point strictly inside its inequalities.

source
FoodWebs.isbounded — Function
isbounded(fc::FeasibleCommunities)

Whether the polytope of fc is bounded, decided from its structure without a solver. With finite bounds or per_capita_bounds it always is. Without efficiency bands, each coordinate sits in one species' equilibrium row, and the polytope is bounded unless some row has unbounded coordinates of both signs that can grow together; a self-regulated top predator whose self-regulation share is below 1 cannot. With efficiency bands, flux is bounded on every link whose band stays below 1, and the polytope is unbounded exactly when links whose band reaches 1 form a feeding loop with no species on it draining a positive minimum self-regulation share.

source
FoodWebs.structurally_singular — Function
structurally_singular(fc::FeasibleCommunities)

Whether the interaction matrix of fc is singular for every point: whether its pattern of entries with a coordinate has no perfect matching of rows to columns. Two top predators without self-regulation that share a single prey are an example: their rows are multiples of the same unit vector.

source
FoodWebs.diagnose — Function
diagnose(fc::FeasibleCommunities)

Necessary conditions for fc to have any point that fail, each as a sentence; empty when none fails. An empty result does not prove the polytope nonempty, which needs a linear program (the PolytopeSamplers.jl extension's isempty). The checks:

  • with efficiency bands, the budget Σ (λ - γ) x_i x_j + Σ σ_i x_i² = x ⋅ r has a non-negative left side, so x ⋅ r ≥ 0 must be possible;
  • a species that eats nothing must be able to have a growth rate of at least 0, to pay for its losses;
  • a species with no predators and no self-regulation must be able to have a growth rate of at most 0, to balance its gains.
source
FoodWebs.CommunityPrior — Type
CommunityPrior(; loss = nothing, gain = nothing, interaction = nothing,
               self_regulation = nothing, growth = nothing)

A prior on the coordinates of a FeasibleCommunities: a univariate distribution for each kind of coordinate, an ExponentialTilt, or nothing for uniform. interaction sets both loss and gain. growth is one distribution for every growth rate or a named tuple (basal = d₁, consumer = d₂) by role. logdensity gives the density restricted to the polytope.

A density on the growth rates times a uniform distribution on the interaction strengths, restricted to the polytope, conditions a joint prior on the equilibrium holding: each rate vector is weighted by the volume of interaction strengths it allows. Drawing the rates first and then sampling the interaction strengths uniformly does not weight by that volume, so the two differ.

sample_communities draws a prior of nothing, ExponentialTilt and Exponential (on coordinates that cannot be negative) exactly on each chord of the sampler.

source
FoodWebs.logdensity — Function
logdensity(prior::CommunityPrior, fc::FeasibleCommunities, θ; atol = 1e-8)

The log density of the point θ of fc under prior, up to a constant: the sum of the log densities of the coordinates with a distribution, and -Inf outside the polytope (to within atol).
