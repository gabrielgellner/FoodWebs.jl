FoodWebs.jl

FoodWebs.jl generates structural food web models, reads empirical food webs, measures their structure and builds random community matrices with their structure, for studying the stability of large ecological communities.

  • Food web models: the cascade, niche and related generators, with Williams & Martinez style acceptance.
  • Empirical food webs: 26 bundled webs with citations, and scripts that download the Web of Life and Mangal webs.
  • Trophic levels and structure: prey-averaged, short-weighted and MacKay trophic levels, trophic incoherence, and the structural properties of Williams & Martinez (2000), tested against their published tables.
  • Motifs: three- and four-species motif censuses, species roles and z-scores against a rewiring null model.
  • Community matrices and stability: random predator–prey Jacobians, spectral and numerical abscissas, and elliptic-law predictions with outliers.
  • Plotting: trophic-level layouts and Makie plots of webs and spectra.

Installation

using Pkg
Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/gabrielgellner/FoodWebs.jl")

FoodWebs.jl requires Julia 1.13. Plotting needs a Makie backend such as CairoMakie.

Conventions

  • Food webs are square binary matrices where web[i, j] == 1 means species j eats species i: prey are rows, predators are columns. PredationMatrix stores a generated web and FoodWeb an empirical one with species names.
  • Connectance is C = L / S^2, with L links among S species (Williams & Martinez 2000).
  • Community matrices use the Jacobian convention: J[i, j] is the effect of species j on the growth of species i, so for predator j and prey i, J[i, j] < 0 < J[j, i].
  • Random generators take an optional leading rng::AbstractRNG, so results are reproducible from a seeded generator.

A first example

using FoodWebs, Distributions, Random

web = niche_network(Xoshiro(1), 30, 0.15)
connectance(web), length(basal_species(web)), length(top_species(web))
(0.14777777777777779, 6, 0)
J = random_predprey(Xoshiro(2), web, Normal(), 0.5)
spectral_abscissa(J), numerical_abscissa(J)
(-0.15912717806491866, 1.3530729761166862)
