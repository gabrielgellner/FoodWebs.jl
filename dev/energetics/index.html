Energy fluxes

fluxes_from_metabolism estimates the biomass flux on every link at equilibrium from biomasses and body masses, following fluxweb (Gauzens et al. 2019). Each consumer loses 0.71 M^-0.25 per unit biomass to metabolism, and its assimilated intake pays for those losses and for what its predators take. Its intake splits among its prey in proportion to their biomass, so the balance is one linear equation per consumer. steady_state_biomass works the other way, passing production up from the basal species.

assimilation_efficiencies gives the efficiency with which each species is assimilated when eaten: 0.545 for plants, 0.906 for animals and 0.158 for detritus (Lang et al. 2017). For a cheddar web it reads the types from the "category" trait.

using FoodWebs

fw = foodweb("cheddar/tl84")
F = fluxes_from_metabolism(fw)
producer = fw.traits["category"] .== "producer"
(herbivory = sum(F[producer, :]), carnivory = sum(F[.!producer, :]))
(herbivory = 0.6728505622365502, carnivory = 0.9774565193257216)

Tuesday Lake records body mass in kg. The metabolic intercept depends on the units of mass and time, so pass intercept to match other units.

Fluxes in feasible communities

Fluxes and biomasses fix the losses of a FeasibleCommunities through fixed_fluxes, and the same efficiencies fix the gains. Each species keeps its growth rate and self-regulation, tied by its equilibrium row, so the polytope has one dimension per self-regulated species.

x = biomass(fw)
fc = FeasibleCommunities(
    fw, x; fixed_fluxes = F, efficiency = assimilation_efficiencies(fw),
    rates = fill((-10.0, 10.0), size(fw, 1))
)
dimension(fc), isbounded(fc)
(56, true)

With self-regulation only from cannibalism, the community matrix is the Jacobian of fluxweb's create.jacob, and its spectral_abscissa is fluxweb's stability.value. fluxweb gives detritus a positive diagonal, which this model has no counterpart for.

FoodWebs.fluxes_from_metabolism — Function
fluxes_from_metabolism(web, x, M; efficiency, intercept = 0.71, exponent = -0.25,
                       preferences = :biomass)
fluxes_from_metabolism(fw::FoodWeb; efficiency = assimilation_efficiencies(fw), kwargs...)

The biomass flux on each feeding link of web at equilibrium, F[prey, predator], the rate at which the prey loses biomass to the predator, from the biomasses x and body masses M (fluxweb, Gauzens et al. 2019). Each consumer j loses X_j = intercept * M_j^exponent per unit biomass to metabolism, and its assimilated intake pays for those losses and for what its predators take:

Σ_i e[i, j] F[i, j] = X_j x_j + Σ_k F[j, k],

with its intake split among its prey by F[i, j] = W[i, j] Σ_k F[k, j]. This is one linear equation per consumer, solved top-down; basal species need no body mass.

  • efficiency: the assimilation efficiency, a number, a vector over species (the efficiency with which each species is assimilated when eaten, as from assimilation_efficiencies), or a matrix over [prey, predator].
  • intercept, exponent: the allometric metabolic rate per unit biomass, numbers or one per species. The defaults are the average over metabolic types in the fluxweb vignette; Brown et al. (2004) give constants by type. The intercept depends on the units of M and of time, so rescale it for other units.
  • preferences: the diet shares W, :biomass (proportional to prey biomass, fluxweb's default), :equal, or a matrix of weights over [prey, predator], zero off the feeding links, that each predator's shares are proportional to; multiply the weights by x to also weight by biomass.

For a FoodWeb the biomasses are biomass and the body masses its "M" trait. Throws an ArgumentError when the balance has no non-negative solution, as when predators take more than their prey's assimilated intake can supply.

Passing F with the same efficiency to FeasibleCommunities as fixed_fluxes fixes the losses and gains of the Lotka–Volterra model. A cannibal's self-regulation σ_i then holds its net cannibalism (1 - e[i, i]) F[i, i] / x_i², and each consumer's growth rate is -X_j plus its self-regulation beyond that. With self-regulation only from cannibalism, the community matrix is fluxweb's create.jacob, J[i, j] = (e[j, i] F[j, i] - F[i, j]) / x_j, for webs without detritus, to which create.jacob gives a positive diagonal.

Gauzens, B. et al. fluxweb: An R package to easily estimate energy fluxes in food webs. Methods Ecol. Evol. 10, 270–279 (2019).

Brown, J. H., Gillooly, J. F., Allen, A. P., Savage, V. M. & West, G. B. Toward a metabolic theory of ecology. Ecology 85, 1771–1789 (2004).

FoodWebs.assimilation_efficiencies — Function
assimilation_efficiencies(types; plant = 0.545, animal = 0.906, detritus = 0.158)
assimilation_efficiencies(fw::FoodWeb; plant, animal, detritus)

The efficiency with which each species is assimilated when eaten, from its resource type, :plant, :animal or :detritus: the fraction of the eaten biomass that pays for the consumer's metabolism and production. The defaults are the means Lang et al. (2017) estimate across consumers.

For a FoodWeb the types come from the cheddar "category" trait: producer is a plant, invertebrate, vert.ecto and vert.endo are animals, and a species with an empty category and no body mass, such as CPOM and FPOM, is detritus. Throws an ArgumentError for any other species, such as the fungi of the Millstream webs, whose types must then be given.

Lang, B., Ehnes, R. B., Brose, U. & Rall, B. C. Temperature and consumer type dependencies of energy flows in natural communities. Oikos 126, 1717–1725 (2017).
