Energy fluxes

fluxes_from_metabolism estimates the biomass flux on every link at equilibrium from biomasses and body masses, following fluxweb (Gauzens et al. 2019). Each consumer loses 0.71 M^-0.25 per unit biomass to metabolism, and its assimilated intake pays for those losses and for what its predators take. Its intake splits among its prey in proportion to their biomass, so the balance is one linear equation per consumer. steady_state_biomass works the other way, passing production up from the basal species.

assimilation_efficiencies gives the efficiency with which each species is assimilated when eaten: 0.545 for plants, 0.906 for animals and 0.158 for detritus (Lang et al. 2017). For a cheddar web it reads the types from the "category" trait.

using FoodWebs

fw = foodweb("cheddar/tl84")
F = fluxes_from_metabolism(fw)
producer = fw.traits["category"] .== "producer"
(herbivory = sum(F[producer, :]), carnivory = sum(F[.!producer, :]))
(herbivory = 0.6728505622365502, carnivory = 0.9774565193257216)

Tuesday Lake records body mass in kg. The metabolic intercept depends on the units of mass and time, so pass intercept to match other units.

Fluxes in feasible communities

Fluxes and biomasses fix the losses of a FeasibleCommunities through fixed_fluxes, and the same efficiencies fix the gains. Each species keeps its growth rate and self-regulation, tied by its equilibrium row, so the polytope has one dimension per self-regulated species.

x = biomass(fw)
fc = FeasibleCommunities(
    fw, x; fixed_fluxes = F, efficiency = assimilation_efficiencies(fw),
    rates = fill((-10.0, 10.0), size(fw, 1))
)
dimension(fc), isbounded(fc)
(56, true)

With self-regulation only from cannibalism, the community matrix is the Jacobian of fluxweb's create.jacob, and its spectral_abscissa is fluxweb's stability.value. fluxweb gives detritus a positive diagonal, which this model has no counterpart for.

FoodWebs.fluxes_from_metabolism — Function
fluxes_from_metabolism(web, x, M; efficiency, intercept = 0.71, exponent = -0.25,
                       preferences = :biomass)
fluxes_from_metabolism(fw::FoodWeb; efficiency = assimilation_efficiencies(fw), kwargs...)

The biomass flux on each feeding link of web at equilibrium, F[prey, predator], the rate at which the prey loses biomass to the predator, from the biomasses x and body masses M (fluxweb, Gauzens et al. 2019). Each consumer j loses X_j = intercept * M_j^exponent per unit biomass to metabolism, and its assimilated intake pays for those losses and for what its predators take:

Σ_i e[i, j] F[i, j] = X_j x_j + Σ_k F[j, k],

with its intake split among its prey by F[i, j] = W[i, j] Σ_k F[k, j]. This is one linear equation per consumer, solved top-down; basal species need no body mass.

  • efficiency: the assimilation efficiency, a number, a vector over species (the efficiency with which each species is assimilated when eaten, as from assimilation_efficiencies), or a matrix over [prey, predator].
  • intercept, exponent: the allometric metabolic rate per unit biomass, numbers or one per species. The defaults are the average over metabolic types in the fluxweb vignette; Brown et al. (2004) give constants by type. The intercept depends on the units of M and of time, so rescale it for other units.
  • preferences: the diet shares W, :biomass (proportional to prey biomass, fluxweb's default), :equal, or a matrix of weights over [prey, predator], zero off the feeding links, that each predator's shares are proportional to; multiply the weights by x to also weight by biomass.

For a FoodWeb the biomasses are biomass and the body masses its "M" trait. Throws an ArgumentError when the balance has no non-negative solution, as when predators take more than their prey's assimilated intake can supply.

Passing F with the same efficiency to FeasibleCommunities as fixed_fluxes fixes the losses and gains of the Lotka–Volterra model. A cannibal's self-regulation σ_i then holds its net cannibalism (1 - e[i, i]) F[i, i] / x_i², and each consumer's growth rate is -X_j plus its self-regulation beyond that. With self-regulation only from cannibalism, the community matrix is fluxweb's create.jacob, J[i, j] = (e[j, i] F[j, i] - F[i, j]) / x_j, for webs without detritus, to which create.jacob gives a positive diagonal.

Gauzens, B. et al. fluxweb: An R package to easily estimate energy fluxes in food webs. Methods Ecol. Evol. 10, 270–279 (2019).

Brown, J. H., Gillooly, J. F., Allen, A. P., Savage, V. M. & West, G. B. Toward a metabolic theory of ecology. Ecology 85, 1771–1789 (2004).

FoodWebs.assimilation_efficiencies — Function
assimilation_efficiencies(types; plant = 0.545, animal = 0.906, detritus = 0.158)
assimilation_efficiencies(fw::FoodWeb; plant, animal, detritus)

The efficiency with which each species is assimilated when eaten, from its resource type, :plant, :animal or :detritus: the fraction of the eaten biomass that pays for the consumer's metabolism and production. The defaults are the means Lang et al. (2017) estimate across consumers.

For a FoodWeb the types come from the cheddar "category" trait: producer is a plant, invertebrate, vert.ecto and vert.endo are animals, and a species with an empty category and no body mass, such as CPOM and FPOM, is detritus. Throws an ArgumentError for any other species, such as the fungi of the Millstream webs, whose types must then be given.

Lang, B., Ehnes, R. B., Brose, U. & Rall, B. C. Temperature and consumer type dependencies of energy flows in natural communities. Oikos 126, 1717–1725 (2017).

Flow networks

A FlowNetwork holds the flows between compartments at steady state, with each compartment's input, export and respiration. Its indices follow enaR (Borrett & Lau 2014): throughflow, total_system_throughflow, total_system_throughput, average_path_length, finn_cycling_index (cycled throughflow over total system throughflow, Finn 1980), ascendency with development capacity and overhead in bits, and mixed_trophic_impact (Ulanowicz & Puccia 1990). The flow-weighted trophic_levels and omnivory_index follow NetIndices (Kones et al. 2009), without cannibalism.

The fluxes of Tuesday Lake give a budget at steady state: producers take in what their consumers eat, and each consumer respires its metabolic losses and exports what it does not assimilate.

e = assimilation_efficiencies(fw)
M = fw.traits["M"]
intake = vec(sum(F; dims = 1))
consumer = intake .> 0
net = FlowNetwork(
    F; inputs = .!consumer .* vec(sum(F; dims = 2)),
    exports = intake .- vec(sum(e .* F; dims = 1)),
    respiration = [consumer[i] ? 0.71 * M[i]^-0.25 * x[i] : 0.0 for i in eachindex(x)],
    names = fw.species,
)
a = ascendency(net)
(
    path_length = average_path_length(net),
    cycling = finn_cycling_index(net),
    relative_ascendency = a.ascendency / a.capacity,
    max_omnivory = maximum(omnivory_index(net)),
)
(path_length = 3.452709671634461, cycling = 0.22378895237139274, relative_ascendency = 0.3265481549972158, max_omnivory = 1.4026458715858776)
FoodWebs.FlowNetwork — Type
FlowNetwork(flows; inputs, exports = 0, respiration = 0, living = true, names, tol = 0.05)

A network of compartments and the flows between them at steady state: flows[i, j] is the flow from compartment i to compartment j, and each compartment has an input from outside, an export and a respiration, numbers or one per compartment. living marks the compartments that are organisms; the others, such as detritus, have trophic level 1 and do not prey on what flows into them. Throws an ArgumentError when some compartment's inputs and inflows differ from its outflows, exports and respiration by more than tol of its outflows, the check of enaR's ssCheck; pass tol = Inf to skip it.

The indices are throughflow, total_system_throughflow, total_system_throughput, average_path_length, finn_cycling_index, ascendency, mixed_trophic_impact, and the flow-weighted trophic_levels and omnivory_index.

FoodWebs.total_system_throughput — Function
total_system_throughput(net::FlowNetwork)

The sum of every flow, inputs, exports and respiration included, TST_p (Ulanowicz 1986), the total of the flow matrix that ascendency extends with the boundary.

FoodWebs.average_path_length — Function
average_path_length(net::FlowNetwork)

The mean number of compartments a unit of input passes through before it leaves, TST / Σ inputs (Finn 1976).

source
FoodWebs.finn_cycling_index — Function
finn_cycling_index(net::FlowNetwork)

The fraction of the total_system_throughflow that cycles, TST_c / TST (Finn 1980), following enaR's enaFlow: compartment i returns a fraction 1 - 1/n_ii of its throughflow to itself, where n_ii is the diagonal of the integral flow matrix (I - G)⁻¹ and G[i, j] is the fraction of j's throughflow that flows to i, so TST_c = Σ_i (1 - 1/n_ii) T_i. NetIndices' FCIb divides TST_c by the total_system_throughput instead, which gives a smaller value.

Finn, J. T. Measures of ecosystem structure and function derived from analysis of flows. J. Theor. Biol. 56, 363–380 (1976).

Finn, J. T. Flow analysis of models of the Hubbard Brook ecosystem. Ecology 61, 562–571 (1980).

source
FoodWebs.ascendency — Function
ascendency(net::FlowNetwork)

Ulanowicz's information measures of the flows, in bits (base-2 logarithms, as in enaR), as a named tuple:

  • capacity: the development capacity C = -Σ T_ij log2(T_ij / T..);
  • ascendency: A = Σ T_ij log2(T_ij T.. / (T_i. T_.j)), the part of C from the organization of the flows;
  • overhead: C - A;
  • diversity: the flow diversity C / T..;
  • mutual_information: the average mutual information A / T...

The sums run over the flow matrix extended with an input row and export and respiration columns, T.. is its total, the total_system_throughput, and T_i. and T_.j are its row and column sums.

Ulanowicz, R. E. Growth and Development: Ecosystems Phenomenology (Springer, 1986).

source
FoodWebs.trophic_levels — Method
trophic_levels(net::FlowNetwork)

The flow-weighted trophic level of each compartment: 1 for non-living compartments and those with no inflow from other compartments, and otherwise 1 plus the mean level of the compartments it takes inflow from, weighted by those inflows (NetIndices' TrophInd). Cannibalistic flows are left out, as in trophic_levels of a web. NetIndices keeps a cannibal's flow to itself in its diet but not in its level, which lowers the level by the share of that flow: for the blue crab of Chesapeake Bay, 3.41 against 3.50 here.

source
FoodWebs.omnivory_index — Function
omnivory_index(net::FlowNetwork)

The omnivory index of each compartment, the variance of the trophic levels of its prey weighted by its diet, Σ_j p_ij (TL_j - (TL_i - 1))² (Pauly et al. 1993, NetIndices' TrophInd): 0 for a compartment that feeds on one trophic level. The diets and levels are those of trophic_levels, without cannibalism.

Pauly, D., Soriano-Bartz, M. L. & Palomares, M. L. D. Improved construction, parametrization and interpretation of steady-state ecosystem models. In Trophic Models of Aquatic Ecosystems, 1–13 (ICLARM, 1993).

source
FoodWebs.mixed_trophic_impact — Function
mixed_trophic_impact(net::FlowNetwork)

The mixed trophic impact M[i, j] of compartment i on compartment j, direct and through every path (Ulanowicz & Puccia 1990), following enaR's enaMTI: M = (I - Q)⁻¹ - I with the direct impacts Q[i, j] = d[i, j] - f[j, i]. The diet share d[i, j] is the flow from i to j over j's throughflow, the benefit of prey i to j; the host share f[j, i] is the flow from j to i over j's throughflow less its respiration, the cost to prey j of i, and is zero when i is not living.

Ulanowicz, R. E. & Puccia, C. J. Mixed trophic impacts in ecosystems. Coenoses 5, 7–16 (1990).
